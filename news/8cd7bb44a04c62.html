美国宇航局关注未来菜单的作物候选

全文

在NASA为未来的月球和火星任务做准备之际，研究人员正在研究一些作物，这些作物可以在远离地球的地方为宇航员提供新鲜食物、营养和多样性。这样的作物品种有助于丰富深空菜单，而本周正值美国庆祝感恩节，这个问题也备受关注。

最近的测试表明，草莓、黄瓜和柠檬罗勒是未来宇航员的潜在选择。太空作物可以吸收二氧化碳、产生氧气并循环利用水。随着载人任务飞得离我们的母星越来越远，这些能力将变得越来越重要。

随着太空作物研究在地球上取得进展，太空方面的能力也在不断拓展。美国宇航局生物与物理科学部的一项新研究即将送往国际空间站，在那里，首个此类原型生长舱将为开发作物生产系统提供关键性见解，这些系统可用于在未来的月球基地栖息地和火星任务中种植此类作物。

甜蜜的太空草莓

草莓是正在考虑用于太空飞行的少数甜味水果之一，它营养丰富、芳香怡人且能自花授粉。

位于佛罗里达州的美国宇航局肯尼迪航天中心的研究人员正在在类似飞行环境的生长舱中种植草莓，以评估其作为太空作物的潜力，而美国宇航局在澳大利亚研究委员会太空植物卓越中心的合作伙伴也在帮助拓展有关太空草莓的知识。

美国宇航局肯尼迪航天中心、墨尔本大学和西澳大利亚大学的研究人员最近将一种野生草莓与美国宇航局的主要草莓品种进行了对比测试，候选品种“Delizz”进行比较，以观察哪种品种在模拟太空条件下生长得更好。

根据9月发表在《空间研究中的生命科学》杂志上的一项研究，Delizz和野生草莓结出的果实大不相同。Delizz结出硕大的红色果实，而野生草莓则因隐性基因突变而结出小小的黄色果实。

但外表并不是一切。从种子开始种植时，野生草莓的花蕾、花朵和果实数量是Delizz的两倍。然而，最终NASA的选择仍位居榜首，因为野生草莓长出了太多的匍匐茎，会占用宝贵的太空舱空间。

黄瓜前景看好

许多作物已经在太空中种植，但迄今为止还没有一种来自葫芦科。黄瓜可能是第一个。

在黄瓜等作物能够种植并在太空中食用，它们必须经过大量测试。由美国宇航局资助的研究人员最近评估了黄瓜在太空飞行条件下的生长、产量和营养情况。

根据8月份发表在《园艺科学》杂志上的一项研究，研究人员发现，虽然这些植物表现出一些叶片变大的迹象，但它们持续不断地结出可食用的果实。

提供各种作物，例如黄瓜等植物，不仅有益于宇航员的饮食和心理健康，还能增加植物生物多样性，从而提高作物在长期任务中成功种植的可能性。

为太空飞行增添滋味

香草可以为太空生活增添滋味，将寡淡的食物变得鲜美可口。

柠檬罗勒是一种具有柑橘香味和味道的可食用香草，目前正在美国宇航局肯尼迪航天中心的测试中取得长足进展。

研究人员报告称，柠檬罗勒已通过初步的空间作物测试，并成功完成了种子消毒，这是在太空飞行前必须进行的步骤，以去除种子上的微生物。柠檬罗勒的后续步骤包括评估其在植物枕中的生长情况，植物枕可以在空间站上容纳植物。

菜单疲劳很快就会成为宇航员的一个问题，但柠檬罗勒等新鲜香草有助于改变将包装餐食转化为新颖且令人满意的食物，为人类在太空中的健康和表现提供支持。

生物与物理科学部

NASA的生物与物理科学部利用太空环境开展在地球上无法进行的研究，从而开创科学发现并推动探索。在极端条件下研究生物和物理现象，使研究人员能够推进在太空走得更远、停留更久所需的基础科学知识，同时造福地球上的生命。

NASA Eyes Crop Candidates for Future Menus

查看英文全文

As NASA prepares for future missions to the Moon and Mars, researchers are studying crops that could provide astronauts with fresh food, nutrition, and variety far from Earth. Such crop variety can help with menus in deep space, something top of mind this week as the nation celebrates Thanksgiving.

Recent testing points to strawberries, cucumbers, and lemon basil as potential options for future explorers. Space crops can absorb carbon dioxide, produce oxygen, and recycle water. These capabilities will become increasingly important as crewed missions extend farther from our home planet.

While space crop research advances on Earth, capabilities are also expanding in space. A new investigation from NASA’s Biological and Physical Sciences Division is set to launch to the International Space Station, where the first-of-its-kind prototype growth chamber will provide critical insights for developing crop production systems that could grow crops like these for future Moon Base habitats and Mars missions.

Sweet space strawberries

Strawberries are among the few sweet fruits under consideration for spaceflight and are nutritious, aromatic, and self-pollinating.

Researchers at NASA’s Kennedy Space Center in Florida are growing strawberries in flight-like growth chambers to evaluate the potential space crop, and NASA partners at The Australian Research Council Centre for Excellence in Plants for Space also are helping expand knowledge of space strawberries.

Researchers at NASA Kennedy, the University of Melbourne, and the University of Western Australia recently tested a wild strawberry against NASA’s leading strawberry candidate, ‘Delizz,’ to see which thrived better under simulated space conditions.

Delizz and the wild strawberry produced very different fruits, according to a study published in September in the journal Life Sciences in Space Research. Delizz grew large, red fruit, while the wild strawberry produced small, yellow fruit due to a recessive genetic mutation.

But looks are not everything. When grown from a seed, the wild strawberry produced twice as many buds, flowers, and fruit as Delizz. Ultimately, however, NASA’s pick kept its top spot, as the wild strawberry grew too many runners that would take up valuable room aboard a spacecraft.

Cucumbers show promise

Many crops have been grown in space, but none so far have come from the gourd family. Cucumbers could be the first.

Before crops like cucumbers can be grown and eaten in space, they must undergo extensive testing. NASA-funded researchers recently evaluated cucumbers for their growth, yield, and nutrition under spaceflight conditions.

The researchers found that while the plants showed some signs of enlarged leaves, they produced a steady stream of edible fruit, according to a study published in August in the journal HortScience.

Offering a variety of crops, such as cucumbers, not only benefits astronauts’ diets and mental well-being, but it also increases plant biodiversity, which can improve the likelihood of crop success on long-duration missions.

Spicing up spaceflight

Herbs can spice up life in space, turning a bland meal into a bold one.

Lemon basil, an edible herb with a citrus scent and taste, is making strides in testing at NASA Kennedy.

Researchers report that lemon basil passed its preliminary round of space crop testing and successfully underwent seed sanitization, which is required before spaceflight to remove microorganisms from the seeds. Lemon basil’s next steps include assessing its growth in plant pillows, which can house plants aboard the space station.

Menu fatigue can quickly become a problem for astronauts, but fresh herbs like lemon basil help transform packaged meals into something new and satisfying, supporting human health and performance in space.

Biological & Physical Sciences Division

NASA’s Biological and Physical Sciences Division pioneers scientific discovery and enables exploration by using space environments to conduct investigations not possible on Earth. Studying biological and physical phenomena under extreme conditions allows researchers to advance the fundamental scientific knowledge required to go farther and stay longer in space, while also benefitting life on Earth.
