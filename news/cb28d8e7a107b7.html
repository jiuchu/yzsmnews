11月21日，北京时间次日凌晨2:24，BepiColombo实现了水星轨道插入（MOI），这是其行星抵达阶段的第二个重要里程碑。在计划中的信号中断之后，来自航天器的信号一经恢复，位于德国达姆施塔特的欧洲航天运营中心（ESOC）的飞行任务控制团队就发现航天器已进入安全模式。

在……期间出现了一个问题推进器点火，导致机动操作比预期提前结束。此后，ESOC的团队一直与主要航天器制造商空客以及欧空局和日本宇宙航空研究开发机构（JAXA）联合任务的同事们昼夜不停地合作调查该问题，并已将其追溯至一个软件故障。

BepiColombo处于安全状态，在水星的稳定轨道上运行——这是在经过八年的漫长旅程，它终于抵达了这颗行星，并正按计划于12月9日在德国（12月10日在日本）分离其两颗科学轨道器。

该任务还实现了另一个“首次”：使用“弱稳定性捕获”进入另一颗行星的轨道。这需要以低于行星探测任务通常速度的较低速度抵达，这是一种此前仅有少数航天器使用过的技术。飞往地球的月球。

“贝皮·哥伦布”号在水星迈出了下一个重要步伐。

11月21日，在水星转移舱（MTM）分离仅两个多月后，水星行星轨道飞行器（MPO）点燃了其四个轴向推进器，以确保其围绕水星的轨道。这是系列推进器点火机动的开端，这些机动将确保并随后逐步降低航天器的轨道。围绕行星。

这次点火标志着“贝皮科伦博”号在MTM后配置中的第一次重大机动。

“贝皮科伦博”号的水星轨道飞行器（MPO）点燃推进器。

随着提前发出的指令，这一夜以一次转向开始，随后是一片寂静。

协调世界时16:51，BepiColombo为机动调整了自身方向，导致与地球的联系按计划暂时中断，而欧洲航天运营中心（ESOC）的飞行任务控制团队则等待着使航天器能够自主执行计划的指令序列。

世界协调时18:24，当“贝皮·哥伦布”号飞掠水星表面上方仅635公里时，其四个轴向推进器按计划开始点火。预计点火将持续37分钟，使航天器的速度减慢到足以确保其围绕该行星的轨道。

通过欧空局的Estrack New Norcia深空天线重新建立联系后，遥测数据显示，机动已被自主中断，航天器已进入安全模式，这是一种保护状态，旨在通过在遇到意外情况时停止所有非必要活动来确保航天器的安全。

“发现Bepi处于安全模式时，就像是拨动了一个开关。每个人都专注于弄清楚发生了什么。”欧空局“贝皮·哥伦布”号飞行运营总监伊格纳西奥·坦科（Ignacio Tanco）说道。任务控制团队立即开始分析航天器和轨道数据。他们的分析表明，点火提前结束，仅实现了计划总速度变化的大约77%。

下一步是确定这对“贝皮·哥伦布”号的轨道意味着什么。重新接收到信号证实了“贝皮·哥伦布”号就在那里，但无法确定它究竟进入了哪条轨道。经过详细的数据分析，当地时间凌晨1点左右，飞行动力学团队通过语音回路给出了明确的答案。

“我们确认已进入水星轨道！”欧空局的BepiColombo飞行动力学经理弗兰克·布德尼克（Frank Budnik）宣布。

抵达水星历经了八年的飞行和数十年的准备。现在，应对意外情况需要今天负责BepiColombo运营的团队具备专业知识和适应能力。

ESOC和空客的团队正在继续调查这一异常情况，他们已将其追溯至一个涉及姿态引导和推进器控制的软件问题。

“贝皮科伦博”号飞往水星的八年旅程在设计时，减速与抵达目的地同样重要。

太阳能电力推进和九次行星飞越重塑了其轨道，并降低了其相对于水星的速度，使航天器能够通过弱引力捕获。

“通过这种弱引力捕获，‘贝皮科伦博’号将保持被捕获状态几个轨道周期，但只是暂时的，”弗兰克解释道。“这就是为什么水星轨道插入（MOI）至关重要。”

在MOI期间，航天器点燃其轴向化学推进器，以刹车并调整其绕水星的轨道。这会将弱捕获转变为一个持久的轨道，最终将“贝皮科伦博”号带入其最终科学轨道。

水星轨道插入并不是一次点火的里程碑。11月21日的推进器点火是未来数月内多次点火中的一次，每次点火都将逐步改变“贝皮·哥伦布”号围绕这颗岩质行星的轨道。这一巧妙方法的背后是欧空局的“贝皮·哥伦布”飞行动力学团队。

那么，为什么需要这么多次点火？

“如果我们减少点火次数，就没有足够的推进剂来完成由于所谓的“重力损失”，我们无法获得想要的结果。”Frank说。

就像在恰到好处的时刻刹车一样，BepiColombo每次点火都有一个“最佳时机”：当它最接近水星时。一次持续数小时的喷射会将航天器带到远超这一最佳点的位置，从而降低机动的效率。通过几次较短时间的点火，BepiColombo可以反复利用这个最佳点，最大限度地减少“重力损失”，并充分利用机载推进剂。

除了燃料效率之外，还有另一个原因使多级点火序列更受青睐。

“每次点火后，飞行动力学团队都会确定已达到的轨道，然后设计下一次机动。因此，该序列不仅推进剂利用率更高，还能让飞行动力学团队根据航天器的实际轨道，”他说。“这是一个动态的序列！每个新的机动操作都基于之前的机动操作。”

MOI序列最初的设计旨在具有灵活性，并能够应对意外事件。由于轨道周期为75小时，而不是原先计划的58小时，剩余的MOI序列已经适应了新的轨道，无需进行额外的点火。这些变化不会影响Mio号于12月9日的脱离，也不会影响MPO号进入其计划的水星科学轨道的能力。

成功进入水星轨道，使我们又向另一个里程碑迈进了一步。

对于JAXA团队来说，MOI是朝着12月9日BepiColombo的两颗科学轨道器分离迈出的激动人心的一步，这两颗轨道器分别是欧空局的水星行星轨道器（MPO）和JAXA的水星磁层轨道器（Mio）。

Mio的保护性遮阳板（MOSIF）将一直附着在MPO上，直至12月16日被弹出。

此后，MPO将通过进一步的推进器点火，在2027年3月之前进入其最终轨道，使科学家们离他们等待多年的时刻更近一步：2027年4月开始科学运行。

“我们近600人的科学团队将很高兴看到BepiColombo通过这一关键里程碑并开始从其内部和表面到周围的空间，揭示了有关水星的更多信息。”

BepiColombo safely in Mercury orbit despite unexpected challenge

查看英文全文

On 21 November, starting at 18:24 UTC, BepiColombo achieved Mercury orbit insertion (MOI), the second major milestone of its planetary arrival phase. As soon as the signal from the spacecraft was re-established after a scheduled loss of contact, the Mission Control Team at the European Space Operations Centre (ESOC) in Darmstadt, Germany, saw that the spacecraft had entered safe mode.

An issue occurred during the thruster burn, causing the manoeuvre to end earlier than expected. Since then, teams at ESOC have been working around the clock with lead spacecraft manufacturer Airbus and colleagues across the joint ESA and Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) mission to investigate the issue, which has been traced to a software glitch.

BepiColombo is safe and in a stable orbit around Mercury – an important milestone after its eight-year journey to the planet – and is on track for the separation of its two science orbiters on 9 December in Germany (10 December in Japan).

The mission also delivered a first: entering orbit around another planet using ‘weak stability capture’. This involves arriving at lower speeds than is typical for planetary missions, and is a technique that has previously only been used by some spacecraft travelling to Earth's moon.

BepiColombo has taken its next big step at Mercury.

On 21 November, little more than two months after separation of the Mercury Transfer Module (MTM), the Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) fired its four axial thrusters to secure its orbit around Mercury. This was the beginning of a series of thruster burn manoeuvres that will secure, and then gradually lower the spacecraft’s orbit around the planet.

The burn marked the first major manoeuvre in BepiColombo’s post-MTM configuration.

BepiColombo's MPO firing its thrusters.

With the commands sent in advance, the night began with a turn followed by silence.

At 16:51 UTC, BepiColombo reoriented itself for the manoeuvre, resulting in a scheduled temporary loss of contact with Earth while the Mission Control Team at ESOC waited for the spacecraft to autonomously carry out the planned sequence of commands.

At 18:24 UTC, as BepiColombo passed just 635 km above Mercury’s surface, its four axial thrusters began firing as planned. The burn was expected to last 37 minutes, slowing the spacecraft enough to secure its orbit around the planet.

When contact was re-established via ESA’s Estrack New Norcia deep-space antenna, telemetry showed that the manoeuvre had been autonomously interrupted and the spacecraft had entered safe mode, a protective state designed to keep the spacecraft safe by ceasing all non-essential activities when it encounters unexpected conditions.

“Upon finding Bepi in safe mode, it was like flipping a switch. Everybody was focused in understanding what had happened,” says Ignacio Tanco, ESA’s BepiColombo Flight Operations Director.

The Mission Control Team immediately began analysing the spacecraft and trajectory data. Their analysis showed that the burn had ended prematurely, achieving around 77% of the total planned velocity change.

The next step was to determine what this meant for BepiColombo’s orbit. Reacquiring the signal confirmed BepiColombo was there, but not exactly which orbit it had secured. After detailed data analysis, a clear answer came in around 01:00 local time through the voice loop from the Flight Dynamics team.

“We confirm Mercury orbit capture!” announced ESA’s BepiColombo Flight Dynamics Manager, Frank Budnik.

Reaching Mercury took eight years of travel and decades of preparation. Navigating the unexpected now calls on the expertise and adaptability of the teams operating BepiColombo today.

Teams at ESOC and Airbus are continuing to investigate the anomaly, which they have traced to a software issue involving attitude guidance and thruster control.

BepiColombo’s eight-year journey to Mercury was designed as much around slowing down as getting there.

Solar electric propulsion and nine planetary flybys reshaped its trajectory and reduced its speed relative to Mercury, allowing the spacecraft to arrive slowly enough via a weak gravity capture.

“With this weak gravity capture, BepiColombo would remain captured for several orbits, but only temporarily,” Frank explains. “This is why Mercury orbit insertion (MOI) is essential.”

During MOI the spacecraft fires its axial chemical thrusters to brake and adjust its orbit around Mercury. This turns weak capture into a lasting orbit that ultimately brings BepiColombo towards its final science orbit.

Mercury orbit insertion is not a single-burn milestone. The thruster burn on 21 November was one of many in the months to come where each one will gradually modify BepiColombo’s orbit around the rocky planet. Behind this ingenious approach is ESA’s BepiColombo Flight Dynamics team.

So, why so many burns?

“If we did it with fewer burns, we wouldn’t have enough propellant to achieve what we want due to what is called “gravity loss”,” Frank says.

Like braking a car at just the right moment, BepiColombo has a ‘sweet spot’ for each burn: when it is at its closest to Mercury. A single, hours-long burn would carry the spacecraft well beyond this optimum point, making the manoeuvre less efficient. Several shorter burns allow BepiColombo to repeatedly take advantage of this sweet spot, minimising ‘gravity losses’ and making the most of the propellant on board.

Fuel efficiency aside, there’s another reason why a multiple burn sequence is preferable.

“After each burn, the Flight Dynamics team determines the achieved orbit to then design the next manoeuvre. So, the sequence is not only more propellant-efficient, it also allows Flight Dynamics to adapt each subsequent burn to the spacecraft’s actual trajectory,” he says. “It's a dynamic sequence! Each new manoeuvre is based on the previous ones.”

The MOI sequence had been originally designed to be flexible and robust to unexpected events. With a 75-hour orbit, rather than the originally planned 58-hour orbit, the remaining MOI sequence has already been adapted to the new trajectory, with no additional burns required. These changes do not affect Mio’s separation on 9 December or MPO’s ability to reach its planned science orbit around Mercury.

The successful Mercury orbit capture now brings another milestone closer.

For the JAXA team, MOI marks an exciting step towards the 9 December separation of BepiColombo’s two science orbiters: ESA’s Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) and JAXA’s Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (Mio).

Mio’s protective sunshield (MOSIF) will remain attached to MPO until it is ejected on 16 December.

From there, further thruster burns will guide MPO towards its final orbit by March 2027, bringing scientists ever closer to the moment they have waited for years: the start of science operations in April 2027.

“Our almost 600-strong science team will be delighted to see BepiColombo pass this key milestone and begin revealing more about Mercury, from its interior and surface to the space around it,” commented Geraint Jones, ESA’s BepiColombo Lead Project Scientist.
