美国宇航局的“欧罗巴快船”号在飞往木星冰冻卫星的途中掠过地球

NASA 行星科学 2026年11月26日 00:00

请在此阅读西班牙文版文章。美国宇航局的“欧罗巴快船”号（Europa Clipper）即将掠过地球，然后将用时数年直飞木星。在那里，这艘轨道飞行器将探测木星的卫星欧罗巴冰壳下方的海洋是否具备生命所需的成分。

全文

NASA的“欧罗巴快船”号即将掠过地球，然后将历时数年直奔木星。在那里，这艘轨道飞行器将探测木卫二冰壳下方的海洋是否具备生命所需的成分。这次飞越将提供一次节省推进剂的加速，并为即将到来的科学数据收集做好准备。

“欧罗巴快艇”号是NASA迄今为止发射到深空的最大型航天器，它将12月3日（星期四），它将飞掠距离地球约1,950至2,133英里（3,140至3,450公里）的空域，并于东部标准时间下午3:14在非洲以西达到最近距离。利用地球的引力将有效地将这艘重达12,800磅（6,000公斤）的航天器弹射至木星，它将于2030年4月开始绕这颗气态巨行星运行。

在飞掠地球时，甚至可以用相对较大的……观测到“欧罗巴快艇”号。对于那些位于最接近轨道附近的地区（尤其是南欧和非洲）的人来说，业余望远镜就足够了。

像这样的飞越被称为“引力助推”，利用行星或卫星的轨道动量来改变航天器的速度和轨道。2025年3月，欧罗巴快艇号在火星表面上方550英里（884公里）处飞掠而过，利用这颗红色星球的引力来减速并改变其绕太阳的轨道。在地球的帮助下，该任务旨在获得速度，并进一步改变其轨道。

“我们将进入地球的引力井，沿着地球的曲线飞行，然后再次飞出。”美国宇航局位于南加州的喷气推进实验室“欧罗巴快艇”项目科学家罗伯特·帕帕拉多（Robert Pappalardo）表示。“这艘航天器能够被地球的引力吸引，并继续前进——现在它的轨道略有变化，飞行速度比以前更快。”

导航员依靠重力助推来节省推进剂，而科学家也将借此机会校准航天器的整套科学仪器。在飞越期间，欧罗巴快艇号的所有九台仪器都将开启并收集数据，这标志着另一个关键的任务里程碑。

各团队将把在飞越期间获得的数据与现有的地球数据进行比较，并根据需要对仪器进行微调。这也是一次重要的学习经历。

“我们正在练习操作的复杂性，但同时也在练习如何协同合作，以取得最佳的科学成果。”喷气推进实验室的“欧罗巴快艇”项目工作人员科学家Erin Leonard表示，“我们现在所做的练习将为我们在欧罗巴的成功奠定基础。”

科学工具箱

对于“欧罗巴快艇”磁强计来说，地球飞越的校准工作尤为重要。该磁强计将研究木星磁场与欧罗巴的相互作用，并旨在确认人们认为存在于该卫星冰壳之下的广阔海洋。

“磁强计测量磁场的变化，但我们需要将这些观测结果与我们已知的事物联系起来，以了解它所测量的实际值，”Pappalardo说。“这次飞越将使我们能够对仪器进行绝对校准，利用它对地球的观测结果来完善在发射前和飞行中进行的其他校准。”

E-THEMIS（欧罗巴快艇热辐射成像系统）仪器将扫描地球，随着航天器接近，每八小时一次。在任务期间飞掠火星时，该仪器被激活，以红外光捕捉到了这颗行星。在木卫二，它将跟踪该卫星的温暖区域，以确定水最接近其表面的位置，甚至可能已经喷发到其表面。

科学团队还将比较来自欧罗巴-UVS（欧罗巴-紫外光谱仪）的地球观测数据，该仪器该任务将研究这颗冰卫星的大气层和表面，而另一项NASA任务“月球勘测轨道器”则将研究月球的大气层和表面。后者将在“欧罗巴快艇”飞越期间将LAMP（莱曼-阿尔法成像项目）仪器对准地球，研究我们星球的大气层最外层——地冕，以更好地了解原子是如何从该区域逸出的。

“木卫二有其独特的大气层晕，由逸出的氢和其他原子组成。”西南研究院的Kurt Retherford表示。他是LAMP、Europa-UVS以及欧空局（欧洲航天局）Juice（木星冰卫星探测器）任务上UVS仪器的首席研究员。“这些由不同航天器在多个有利位置进行的地球观测是一次很好的测试，检验我们如何在木星上进行同样的工作。”

该团队正在他们渴望了解仪器的性能，并分享他们的观察结果。

“当我们真正能够收集对仪器来说真正有意义的数据，并能够与其他人分享时，这真是令人兴奋不已，”伦纳德说。“这是地球，每个人都明白这些数据的意义。想象一下，它将能够向您展示有关木卫二的哪些信息。”

当“欧罗巴快艇”号接近其目的地，它将利用木星的另一颗卫星木卫三的引力来减速，并被木星的引力捕获。在随后飞越木卫二的过程中，该航天器将确定其冰壳的厚度，研究木卫二的成分，并对其地质特征进行描述。该任务的发现将帮助科学家更好地了解地球以外可能存在宜居世界的可能性。NASA JPL 由位于加利福尼亚州帕萨迪纳的加州理工学院管理，与位于马里兰州劳雷尔的约翰斯·霍普金斯大学应用物理实验室（APL）合作，为位于华盛顿的 NASA 科学任务局牵头开展“欧罗巴快船”任务。航天器主体由APL与JPL以及位于马里兰州格林贝尔特的NASA戈达德太空飞行中心、NASA马歇尔太空飞行中心合作设计。位于阿拉巴马州亨茨维尔的马歇尔太空飞行中心，以及位于弗吉尼亚州汉普顿的NASA兰利研究中心。NASA马歇尔太空飞行中心的行星任务项目办公室负责“欧罗巴快艇”任务的项目管理。

NASA’s Europa Clipper Zooming by Earth on Way to Jupiter’s Icy Moon

查看英文全文

NASA’s Europa Clipper will soon swing past Earth before making a yearslong beeline for Jupiter, where the orbiter will probe whether the ocean beneath its moon Europa’s icy shell has the ingredients for life. The flyby will offer a propellant-saving boost and an opportunity to prepare for the science-gathering that lies ahead.

The largest spacecraft NASA has launched into deep space, Europa Clipper will come within about 1,950 to 2,133 miles (3,140 to 3,450 kilometers) of Earth on Thursday, Dec. 3, with its closest approach west of Africa at 3:14 p.m. EST. Harnessing our planet’s gravity will effectively slingshot the 12,800-pound (6,000-kilogram) spacecraft toward Jupiter, where it will begin orbiting the gas giant in April 2030.

As it flies by Earth, Europa Clipper will even be observable with relatively large hobby telescopes for those near the closest approach trajectory, notably in southern Europe and in Africa.

Called gravity assists, flybys like these take advantage of a planet or moon’s orbital momentum to change the spacecraft’s speed and trajectory. In March 2025, Europa Clipper sailed 550 miles (884 kilometers) above Mars’ surface, using the Red Planet’s gravitational pull to pump the brakes and reshape its orbit around the Sun. With Earth’s help, the mission aims to gain speed and alter its trajectory a bit more.

“We’re coming down into the gravity well of Earth, following its curve, and shooting out again,” said Robert Pappalardo, Europa Clipper’s project scientist at NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory in Southern California. “The spacecraft is able to get pulled by the gravity of the planet and continue on its way — now with a slightly changed trajectory and moving faster than it was before.”

While navigators are relying on the gravity assist to help save propellant, scientists will also take advantage of the opportunity to calibrate the spacecraft’s suite of science instruments. All nine of Europa Clipper’s instruments will be turned on and collecting data during the flyby, marking another crucial mission milestone.

Teams will compare the data they acquire during the flyby with existing Earth data and fine-tune their instruments as needed. It’s an important learning experience as well.

“We’re practicing the intricacies of the operation, but we’re also practicing how to work together to produce the best science,” said JPL’s Erin Leonard, Europa Clipper project staff scientist. “The things we practice now will set us up for success at Europa.”

Science toolbox

The calibration efforts of the Earth flyby are especially important for the Europa Clipper Magnetometer, which will study how Jupiter’s magnetic field interacts with Europa and aims to confirm the presence of the vast ocean believed to be beneath the moon’s icy shell.

“The magnetometer measures variations in the magnetic field as we’re orbiting Jupiter, but we need to pin these observations to something we know to understand the actual value that it’s measuring,” Pappalardo said. “The flyby will enable us to calibrate the instrument absolutely, using its observations at Earth to refine other calibrations done before launch and in flight.”

The E-THEMIS (Europa Clipper Thermal Emission Imaging System) instrument will scan Earth every eight hours as the spacecraft approaches. When it was activated during the mission’s Mars flyby, the instrument captured the planet in infrared. At Europa, it will track the moon’s warm regions to determine where water is closest to — or may even have erupted onto — its surface.

The science team also will compare Earth observations from the Europa-UVS (Europa-Ultraviolet Spectrograph), an instrument that will study the icy moon’s atmosphere and surface, to those of another NASA mission, Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter. The latter will train its LAMP (Lyman-Alpha Mapping Project) instrument on Earth during Europa Clipper’s flyby to study our planet’s geocorona, the outermost part of the atmosphere, to better understand how atoms are escaping that region.

“Europa has its own type of atmospheric corona composed of escaping hydrogen and other atoms,” said Southwest Research Institute’s Kurt Retherford, principal investigator for LAMP, the Europa-UVS, and the UVS instrument on the ESA (European Space Agency) Juice (Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer) mission. “These Earth observations made at multiple vantage points by different spacecraft are a good test run for how we aim to do the same at Jupiter.”

The team is eager to see how the instruments perform — and to share what they see.

“It’s super-exciting when we actually do get to collect data that’s really meaningful to the instruments, and get to share it with other people,” Leonard said. “This is Earth, and everyone understands what that data means. Imagine what it’s going to be able to show you about Europa.”

When Europa Clipper approaches its destination, it will use the gravitational pull of another Jovian moon, Ganymede, to slow down and be captured by Jupiter’s gravity. During its subsequent flybys of Europa, the spacecraft will determine the thickness of its icy shell, investigate Europa’s composition, and characterize its geology. The mission’s findings will help scientists better understand the potential for habitable worlds beyond our own.

Managed by Caltech in Pasadena, California, NASA JPL leads the development of the Europa Clipper mission in partnership with the Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory (APL) in Laurel, Maryland, for NASA’s Science Mission Directorate in Washington. The main spacecraft body was designed by APL in collaboration with JPL and NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center in Greenbelt, Maryland, NASA’s Marshall Space Flight Center in Huntsville, Alabama, and NASA’s Langley Research Center in Hampton, Virginia. The Planetary Missions Program Office at NASA Marshall executes program management of the Europa Clipper mission.
